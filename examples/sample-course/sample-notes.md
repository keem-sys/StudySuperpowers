# Sample lecture notes: Introduction to Ecology

These original notes are fictional and intentionally short. They define the terms used in the [course outline and evaluation cases](course-outline.md).

## Levels of ecological organisation

- A **population** is individuals of the same species living in the same area at the same time. Example: all rabbits in one grassland.
- A **community** is the populations of different species living and interacting in an area. A grassland community could include rabbits, grasses, insects, and foxes.
- An **ecosystem** includes a community and the non-living parts of its environment, such as water, soil, light, and temperature, together with their interactions.

These terms describe different scopes. A community includes living populations; an ecosystem includes the community plus abiotic environmental factors and interactions.

## Limiting factors

A **limiting factor** is a resource or environmental condition that restricts population growth or abundance. Food, water, nesting sites, disease, and temperature can act as limiting factors. Which factor matters most depends on the organism and conditions.

If edible plant material becomes scarce, it can limit a rabbit population because food availability affects survival and reproduction. This does not by itself tell us the exact population change or how quickly it occurs.

## Carrying capacity

The **carrying capacity** of an environment for a population is the largest population it can support over time under the current conditions and resource availability. It is not necessarily a fixed number: changes in food, water, habitat, or other conditions can change it.

If a drought reduces plant food, the grassland may support fewer rabbits over time, so its carrying capacity for rabbits may decrease. The population may adjust over time through changes in survival and reproduction. These notes do not provide numerical predictions.
