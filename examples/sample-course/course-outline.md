# Sample course: Introduction to Ecology

This fictional mini-course is original material created to test StudySuperpowers workflows. It is not based on a real university syllabus.

## Course scope

This short unit introduces population, community, ecosystem, limiting factors, and carrying capacity. Use the linked [sample notes](sample-notes.md) as its source.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this mini-course, a student should be able to:

1. Distinguish a population, a community, and an ecosystem.
2. Explain how a limiting factor affects population growth.
3. Define carrying capacity and describe how a change in resources can alter it.
4. Apply these concepts to a simple ecosystem scenario.

## Practice question

**Question (5 marks):** A drought reduces the amount of edible plant material in a grassland. Explain how this could affect the rabbit population's carrying capacity and population size over time. Use the terms *limiting factor* and *carrying capacity* accurately.

### Example marking rubric

This fictional rubric is supplied as an official rubric for this sample question only.

| Criterion | Marks |
| --- | ---: |
| Identifies available plant food as a limiting factor/resource | 1 |
| Defines carrying capacity as the largest population the environment can support over time | 1 |
| Explains that less food can lower the grassland's carrying capacity for rabbits | 1 |
| Connects food shortage to reduced survival and/or reproduction | 1 |
| Describes a plausible population decline or adjustment over time, without claiming an immediate exact outcome | 1 |
| **Total** | **5** |

Award partial credit for a correct idea expressed imprecisely. Do not require the exact wording of the criteria. The rubric does not specify exact timing or population numbers.

## Evaluation scenarios

Use these cases to check whether a workflow follows its stated behavior. Expected responses below are for evaluation, not answers to show before a student attempts a question.

### Correct answer

**Student response:** “The plants are a limiting factor because rabbits need them as food. If the drought reduces food, the environment can support fewer rabbits, so carrying capacity falls. Survival or reproduction may decrease, and the rabbit population may decline over time.”

**Expected behavior:** Credit all five rubric criteria, explain each point briefly, and suggest 5/5. Do not imply the population instantaneously reaches a specific number.

### Incomplete answer

**Student response:** “The drought makes edible plants a limiting factor.”

**Expected behavior:** Award the point for identifying edible plants as a limiting factor. The response does not meet the other four criteria, so suggest 1/5 and explain the missing definition and causal links.

### Incorrect answer

**Student response:** “The drought increases carrying capacity because fewer plants means less competition, so the rabbit population grows.”

**Expected behavior:** Identify the reversed relationship: edible plants are food, and reduced availability can reduce the carrying capacity. Explain that reduced competition does not establish more resources, and invite a retry or give targeted feedback. Do not reward the unsupported conclusion.

### Ambiguous source material

**Scenario:** A hypothetical uploaded note says, “The grassland's carrying capacity is 200 rabbits,” while another supplied page says, “The current estimate is 120 rabbits.” Neither page has a date or context.

**Expected behavior:** Point out the conflict, avoid choosing one as authoritative, ask which source/version applies, and avoid scoring an answer that depends on the disputed number. The provided rubric contains no expected rabbit count.

### Missing marking criteria

**Scenario:** The practice question and a student's answer are available, but no memorandum or rubric is supplied.

**Expected behavior:** State that no official criteria were supplied. If the available marks are known, provide transparent question-specific estimated criteria and label both criteria and any suggested score as estimates. If the mark total is unknown, give qualitative feedback or ask for it instead of inventing a total.
