# Active recall

## Purpose

Help the student retrieve and apply course knowledge without seeing the answer first, then use feedback and varied questions to identify topics worth revisiting.

## When to use

Use this workflow after the student has studied material and wants to test recall, reasoning, or application. It is not a substitute for learning an unfamiliar topic from scratch.

## Required and optional inputs

**Required:** A course topic, unit, or learning objective to practise.

**Optional:** Course notes or slides, learning outcomes, question format, difficulty, session length, and topics the student wants to revisit.

Use supplied materials and outcomes as the primary source. If neither is provided, ask whether the student wants a general-knowledge quiz and label its scope as not verified against their course.

## Step-by-step procedure

1. **Set scope.** Confirm the topic and relevant learning objectives. Ask for missing materials if course-specific coverage matters.
2. **Choose a mix.** Prepare questions across the supplied objectives and vary difficulty and cognitive demand: for example, recall, explanation, comparison, and application. Do not show the answer key in advance.
3. **Ask one question.** Present one clear question and wait for the student's response. Do not bundle several numbered questions or reveal the solution in the same turn.
4. **Assess the response.** Comment separately on correctness, completeness, and reasoning. Cite or name the source when possible. Distinguish a factual error from an incomplete explanation.
5. **Give targeted feedback.** Explain the key idea without shaming. When the response is substantially incorrect or incomplete, give the student a chance to retry or provide a hint before revealing a full solution. Respect a request to see the explanation.
6. **Adapt practice.** Track concepts that are secure versus uncertain within this session. Revisit weak concepts using a different wording or application. Increase difficulty gradually; do not infer mastery from one correct response.
7. **Continue deliberately.** Ask one question at a time until the student stops or the agreed objectives have been sampled. If evidence is thin, say which objectives remain untested.
8. **Summarise.** Give a concise revision summary listing covered objectives, strengths, gaps to revisit, and one or more next practice steps. Do not describe an objective as mastered on the basis of a single response.

## Expected output

An interactive question-and-feedback session with varied difficulty, one question at a time, source-grounded evaluation, opportunities to retry, targeted revisits, and a concise end-of-session revision summary.

## Guardrails and failure handling

- Never reveal the answer before the student has attempted the question, unless they explicitly ask to skip the attempt.
- Do not treat a correct guess or a single correct answer as proof of mastery.
- Do not claim that a generated question is an official or likely exam question.
- If a source is unreadable, ambiguous, or contradictory, say so and narrow or pause the question rather than inventing a course answer.
- If an answer includes a specific claim the supplied material does not establish, identify it as unsupported rather than automatically false. Assess any supported parts separately and do not invent a course correction.
- Mark questions based on unsupplied knowledge as general practice, not course-verified coverage.
- Keep feedback proportionate and explain why an answer is incomplete or incorrect.

## Evaluation criteria

A successful session uses supplied materials and objectives as its source; includes varied, relevant question demands; asks one question and waits; distinguishes correctness, completeness, and reasoning; offers retry or hints when useful; revisits weak ideas differently; avoids premature mastery claims; and ends with a concise, evidence-based revision summary.

## Usage example

> Use `active-recall` for the ecosystem and population objectives in my notes. Give me a mix of recall and application questions, one at a time. Wait for each answer, and let me retry if I miss an important point.
