# Active recall

## Purpose

Help students retrieve and apply course knowledge without seeing the answer first. Use their responses to identify knowledge gaps, adapt practice, and guide revision.

## When to use

Use this skill after a student has studied material and wants to test recall, understanding, reasoning, or application. It is not a substitute for learning an unfamiliar topic from scratch.

## Required and optional inputs

**Required:** A course topic, unit, or learning objective.

**Optional:** Course notes or slides, learning outcomes, preferred question format, difficulty, session length, and topics to revisit.

Use supplied course materials and learning outcomes as the primary source. If neither is provided, ask whether the student wants general-knowledge practice and clearly label it as not verified against their course.

## Step-by-step procedure

### 1. Establish scope

Identify the topic and relevant learning objectives. Ask for missing materials when course-specific coverage matters. If the scope is unclear, ask one concise clarifying question.

### 2. Plan varied practice

Prepare questions that sample the available objectives and vary in difficulty and cognitive demand, such as recall, explanation, comparison, and application. Do not reveal the answer key in advance.

### 3. Ask one question at a time

Present one clear question and wait for the student's response. Do not bundle multiple questions or reveal the solution in the same turn. Do not provide hints that effectively disclose the answer before the student attempts it.

### 4. Assess the response

Evaluate correctness, completeness, and reasoning separately where relevant. Distinguish a factual error from an incomplete explanation, and recognise correct parts of a partially correct answer. Refer to the supplied source when possible.

### 5. Give useful feedback

Explain what the student got right, what is missing or incorrect, and why. If the answer is substantially incorrect or incomplete, offer a hint or an opportunity to retry before revealing the full explanation. Respect requests to skip the retry or see the answer.

Keep feedback constructive and proportionate to the question.

### 6. Adapt the next question

Use responses within the current session to track concepts that appear secure, uncertain, or not yet tested. Revisit weak concepts with a different question or application. Adjust difficulty gradually, and do not infer mastery from a single correct answer or an unsupported guess.

### 7. Manage the session

Continue one question at a time until the agreed objectives have been sampled or the student chooses to stop. Keep the session proportionate to the requested scope and length. If coverage is incomplete, state which objectives remain untested rather than implying comprehensive coverage.

### 8. Summarise progress

At the end of the session, provide a concise revision summary covering:
- Objectives and concepts practised.
- Demonstrated strengths, supported by the student's responses.
- Errors, uncertainties, and gaps to revisit.
- Objectives that remain untested.
- Recommended next steps or follow-up practice.

Describe mastery cautiously. A single correct answer is evidence of success on that question, not proof of lasting mastery.

## Guardrails

- Do not reveal an answer before the student attempts the question unless they explicitly request the answer or choose to skip the attempt.
- Ask only one question per response during the interactive practice exchange.
- Do not claim generated questions are official, guaranteed, or likely exam questions.
- If source material is unreadable, ambiguous, or contradictory, explain the limitation and narrow or pause the affected question rather than inventing a course answer.
- If the material does not support a specific claim, identify it as unsupported rather than automatically false. Assess supported parts separately.
- Label practice based on unsupplied knowledge as general practice, not course-verified coverage.
- Do not claim objectives have been comprehensively covered when only some have been tested.
- Do not claim mastery or long-term retention without sufficient evidence.

## Evaluation criteria

A successful session uses the appropriate source and scope, varies question type and difficulty, asks one question at a time, waits for responses, evaluates correctness and reasoning, offers useful hints or retries, revisits weak concepts, reports untested objectives, and provides an evidence-based revision summary without overstating mastery.

## Usage example

> Use `active-recall` for the ecosystem and population objectives in my notes. Give me a mix of recall and application questions, one at a time. Wait for each answer, explain any gaps, and let me retry before revealing the solution when useful. Finish with a summary of my strengths and what I should revise.
